Available Student Projects

We develop X-ray crystallography methods at the frontier of what's possible - some for the first time anywhere, others bringing world-leading capabilities to Australia. We want ambitious, high-performing students to help build and apply these methods to important biomedical and chemical targets.

PhD Projects

Offers have been made for both of our funded PhD scholarships (Projects 1 and 2), pending enrolment. We can support SUPRA/RTPS applications for the remaining projects - see How to Apply.

1. Establishing time-resolved serial crystallography at the Australian Synchrotron

This project will develop and advance time-resolved serial microcrystallography (TR-SSX) at the MX beamlines of the Australian Synchrotron - a capability not yet available there. It spans X-ray diffraction physics and beam characterisation, sample-delivery instrumentation, data-acquisition scripting and analysis code, and crystal growth and structure refinement, applied to biological molecules and hybrid materials like MOFs. Collaboration with ANSTO, RMIT and Monash. AINSE top-up eligible (see below).

Physics or biophysics degree required. Relevant engineering backgrounds (mechanical, controls, or data-acquisition) also welcome.

2. Molecular movies of DsbA

Capture the dynamics of drug binding to disulfide bond-forming enzyme A (DsbA) with time-resolved serial crystallography. Study the fundamentals of oxidative protein folding to understand disulphide bond formation in bacteria. DsbA is a high-value target in the fight against antibiotic resistance.

Biochemistry, biophysics or related degree required, ideally with structural biology experience. Physics or relevant engineering (mechanical, controls, or data-acquisition) backgrounds also welcome.

BiochemistryPhysicsOffer made - pending enrolment

3. Synchrotron Nanocrystallography

Develop a new analysis technique to obtain protein structures from nanocrystal diffraction collected via serial crystallography methods. Nanocrystallography opens up new opportunities for time-resolved studies of molecular interactions at the atomic scale. Our approach is based on advanced statistical diffraction analysis techniques (fluctuation scattering). Involves nanocrystal synthesis, testing sample delivery methods, and/or analysing diffraction using Python and other software. Experiments conducted at the MX beamlines of the Australian Synchrotron, in collaboration with A/Prof Andrew Martin (RMIT) and CFEL/DESY (Germany). This PhD scholarship is at RMIT in Andrew Martin's group and is eligible for an AINSE PhD top-up. See: RMIT scholarship listing.

Physics or biophysics degree required.

PhysicsPhD scholarship available at RMITAINSE top-up eligible

4. Structural Characterisation of SPL Inhibitors

Contribute to neurodegenerative disease drug development. Investigate the binding interface of Sphingosine 1-Phosphate Lyase (SPL) against small molecule inhibitors, a route to slowing disease progression in conditions such as Multiple Sclerosis and Alzheimer's Disease. You will express and purify SPL protein and use X-ray crystallography to solve the SPL-inhibitor complex structure.

Biochemistry or related degree required. Apply via the School of Health Sciences for a more biomedical/neuroscience focus, or via SoSCET for a more structural biology / molecular interactions focus - email us to discuss which suits you.

Biochemistry

5. Simulating diffraction experiments for novel compact X-ray sources

A flexible, largely computational project developing simulations of diffraction experiments using novel compact X-ray sources - very different in their beam properties from undulators or XFELs (e.g. very high bandwidth, high divergence, large collimated beams, or tiny highly-divergent beams, with bandwidth that varies with angle). The goal is a simulation pipeline to quantify what experiments become possible with such beams. Collaboration with the ASU Compact XFEL team.

Physics or biophysics degree required, with an interest in coding and diffraction.

Honours / Masters Projects (Physics and Biochemistry)

Send your CV and academic transcript, and indicate which project(s) interest you, when contacting us. High-performing Honours students can upgrade to continue as a Masters by Research. These run alongside the PhD projects above as bite-sized versions - not a shadow project, a scoped-down piece of the same problem.

1. Room-temperature crystallography of the bacterial enzyme DsbA

Two options, both linked to PhD Project 2 above:

Option A: structural studies of a non-E. coli DsbA source, working towards drug development via serial (room-temperature) and time-resolved crystallography.

Option B: E. coli DsbA (as in PhD Project 2), working towards understanding redox folding - includes designing peptides to simulate DsbB, protein-peptide binding simulations / molecular docking, and crystallography.

Biochemistry or related degree required.

Biochemistry

2. Microcrystal sample delivery systems for serial and time-resolved crystallography

Develop new microcrystal sample delivery systems for serial and time-resolved crystallography (e.g. tape-drive, high-viscosity injection). You can focus on hardware or on experiment optimisation. This work is all towards enabling time-resolved crystallography - a new method for the Australian Synchrotron.

Physics or relevant engineering (mechanical, controls, or data-acquisition) backgrounds also welcome.

Physics

3. Serial Crystallography of Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOFs)

A new and exciting area with potential industrial applications. Explore how serial crystallography can be applied to MOFs, porous materials used in gas storage, catalysis, and sensing (see Chemistry Nobel Prize 2025). Combines structural chemistry with modern X-ray methods, in collaboration with the University of Melbourne and the Australian Synchrotron.

Physics and/or chemistry degree required.

PhysicsChemistry

4. Scientific computing for serial crystallography

Build and modernise the tools that make our beamtime work, for students with a strong interest in coding, modelling, or data analysis - no lab work required. Sub-topics include: modernising DatView (Python), including a possible Julia rewrite, and extending it to new capabilities at the MX beamlines (in-tray screening, wedge data merging automation); building live data-quality feedback during beamtime (hit rates, indexing rates, saturation, plotted in real time); an easy-to-use interactive mask-maker tool; and image analysis tools to quickly pull sample images from raw data files. These projects are eligible for AINSE top-ups and connect you to the Scientific Computing team at the Australian Synchrotron. Email us to discuss options.

Computational

Undergrad / Capstone / Summer & Winter Projects

If you're interested in scientific computing and smaller projects (e.g. one semester), we might have opportunities in our group or with the Scientific Computing team at the Australian Synchrotron. Several sub-topics from "Scientific computing for serial crystallography" (Honours Project 4 above) are also available as short-term, WIL, or Capstone projects - some with small scholarships attached. The example below shows what one such sub-topic, the DatView upgrade, might include.

1. Example: modernising the DatView GUI

The DatView GUI, developed for serial crystallography data analysis, visualises and queries large tabulated datasets. A project here might improve its performance (data loading from text/HDF5, memory use, export speed) and broaden its applicability, potentially including a rewrite in a faster language (e.g. Julia). Scalable from a short project up to Honours or Masters. See the paper and GitHub repo.

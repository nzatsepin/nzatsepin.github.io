Macromolecular crystallography · teaching module

Radiation damage, from photon to lattice

Every absorbed X-ray photon deposits ~keV into a crystal that is held together by interactions worth a few kJ/mol. The damage proceeds in two overlapping regimes: specific (site-selective chemistry, visible in difference maps at low dose) and global (loss of diffracting power, visible in the data statistics). Dose, in grays (1 Gy = 1 J kg−1), is the metric against which both are tracked.1

01Primary interactions

At 12.4 keV in protein crystals only a small fraction of interacting photons scatter elastically; most are absorbed photoelectrically. Energy is deposited by the electrons that result, not by the photon itself.

02Electron cascade and radiolysis

The crystal is ~30–70 % solvent, so most energy is deposited in water. Ionisation and excitation of water produce the radicals that attack the protein (indirect damage); direct ionisation of the protein produces holes and electrons in situ.

Energetics

  • Photoelectron energy ≈ Eγ − EB; K-edges: C 0.28, N 0.41, O 0.54, S 2.47 keV. A 12.4 keV photon on O yields an ≈ 11.9 keV photoelectron.
  • Auger electrons from C/N/O: ≈ 0.25–0.5 keV, ranges of a few nm.
  • Each 12 keV photoelectron produces ~500 further ionisations (≈ 25–30 eV per ion pair).17 APPROX
  • Photoelectron range at 12.4 keV ≈ 2–4 µm in protein crystals, so microbeams and microcrystals can lose part of the dose by escape.15,16
  • Low-energy (< 15 eV) secondary electrons break bonds by dissociative electron attachment.18

03Radical mobility: 100 K versus room temperature

Cryocooling does not prevent radical formation. It stops diffusion of OH• and other heavy radicals, but electrons (and, more slowly, holes) still migrate by tunnelling and hopping to electron-affinic sites. This is why metal centres and disulfides are reduced at 100 K.1,8

Live tally

100 K

  • OH• immobile in vitreous solvent below ~110 K. APPROX
  • Electrons migrate to metal centres and disulfides; holes to electron-rich sites such as carboxylates.
  • Damage rate effectively independent of dose rate over synchrotron ranges.20
  • H₂ accumulates; further cooling to ~15–50 K gives a modest additional gain, magnitude system-dependent.14 DEBATED

Room temperature

  • OH•, H• and e⁻aq diffuse through solvent channels and react with surface residues.
  • Global dose limits fall by roughly 30–100× relative to 100 K.21,22 APPROX
  • Dose-rate effects appear: very high dose rates can partly outrun radical diffusion.23,24

04Specific damage

Site-selective changes seen as negative Fo(Dn) − Fo(D1) difference density. Typical order of susceptibility at 100 K: metal reduction ≫ disulfide cleavage > Glu/Asp decarboxylation > Tyr O–H loss > Met C–S cleavage.6,7,8 Within each class, susceptibility depends on environment (active sites and strained bonds are hit first) and does not correlate with solvent accessibility.10

1 · reduction (e⁻)

Metal centres

Fe(III) + e⁻ → Fe(II)
Cu(II) + e⁻ → Cu(I)
Mn(IV/III) → Mn(II)

Fastest process. Heme Fe(III) reduction observed by online UV-vis at a small fraction of a data set;12 the PSII Mn₄CaO₅ cluster is substantially reduced at doses used for standard data collection.13 Validate redox state with online spectroscopy or XAS. Onset typically < 1 MGy. SYSTEM-DEPENDENT

2 · reduction (e⁻)

Disulfide bonds

RSSR + e⁻ → [RSSR]•⁻
[RSSR]•⁻ → RS• + RS⁻

Electron capture forms the disulfide radical anion, with ~0.7 Å bond elongation and a ~400 nm absorption band in crystals.9 Further dose cleaves the bond; Sγ density splits or disappears.7,8

3 · oxidation (h⁺)

Glu / Asp decarboxylation

RCOO⁻ − e⁻ → RCOO•
RCOO• → R• + CO₂

Hole or OH• oxidation of the carboxylate followed by CO₂ loss; carboxyl density fades first.6,7 Active-site and crystal-contact acids are most sensitive.10 CO₂ may remain trapped near the site at 100 K. CASE-DEPENDENT

4–5 · later onset

Met C–S and Tyr O–H

Met: Cγ–Sδ or Sδ–Cε scission
Tyr: loss of OH density

Met C–S cleavage and Tyr hydroxyl loss are reported at higher doses than disulfide and carboxylate damage.6 Relevant for Se-Met phasing: Se sites decay with dose, which can degrade or (RIP) be exploited for phasing.1

05Global damage

Accumulated specific damage and lattice disorder reduce diffracting power, highest resolution first. Model: mean intensity at resolution d decays as ⟨I⟩ = ⟨I⟩₀ exp(−ln2 · D / (H d)), with H = 10 MGy Å⁻¹, so D½ ≈ 10d MGy.4,5,11

Observables

  • Intensity loss, starting at high resolution; I/σ(I) in the outer shell falls first.
  • Wilson B rises roughly linearly with dose; rate varies between crystals.3
  • Unit-cell volume expands, typically by a few tenths of a percent to ~1 %; rate is not transferable between crystals, so not a reliable dose metric.25,26 APPROX
  • Mosaicity often increases; not universal.
  • Scaling B (per-batch relative B in AIMLESS/XSCALE) becomes increasingly negative with frame number.
  • Rd rises with frame separation (Diederichs).27
  • Rising Rmerge, falling CC½ in the outer shell.

06Practical consequences

  • Plan dose, not exposure time. Calculate with RADDOSE-3D for the actual beam profile, crystal shape and composition.28
  • Spread dose. Helical/vector scans, multiple positions, multi-crystal merging, serial crystallography.
  • Collect redox-sensitive sites first, or composite structures from low-dose wedges of many crystals; verify with online spectroscopy.12,13
  • Microbeams on microcrystals can gain from photoelectron escape, larger at higher photon energy.15,16
  • Scavengers: mixed results at 100 K; some benefit reported at RT.22 CASE-DEPENDENT
  • XFEL femtosecond pulses outrun global damage ("diffraction before destruction"),30 but metal sites can still be affected within longer pulses.31

Diagnosing specific damage in your data

  • Process the first and last wedges separately; inspect Fo(late) − Fo(early) at disulfides, metals, Glu/Asp, Met.
  • Look for negative peaks on Sγ, carboxyl O, metal ligands; positive peaks at alternative S positions.
  • Check metal–ligand distances against the expected oxidation state.
  • Track scaling B and Rd versus frame; truncate where the outer-shell CC½ or I/σ(I) degrades.

Ewald construction schematic

Rotate a crystal, or a single molecule, in the beam. Wherever reciprocal space meets the Ewald sphere, X-rays scatter to the detector. The geometry is the same for both; a crystal concentrates the signal into Bragg peaks.

Bragg's law

λ = 2 dhkl sin θ

θ is the glancing angle between the incident beam and the lattice planes (hkl); the diffracted beam leaves at 2θ from the incident direction. Higher orders are written as separate reflections: the n-th order of (hkl) is (nh nk nl), with dnh,nk,nl = dhkl/n [1, 2].

Reciprocal lattice

d*hkl = ha* + kb* + lc*,  |d*hkl| = 1/dhkl

a* = (b×c)/V and cyclic. d*hkl is normal to the planes (hkl). Rotating the crystal rotates the reciprocal lattice rigidly about its origin O [3, 4].

Ewald construction

k − k0 = q,  |k| = |k0| = 1/λ

The sphere of radius 1/λ is centred at C = O − k0, so O lies on its surface. Elastic scattering samples reciprocal space only on this sphere. For a crystal, intensity is non-zero only at q = d*hkl, which is equivalent to Bragg's law with sin θ = λ|d*|/2. For a single molecule, the continuous transform |F(q)|² is sampled everywhere on the sphere. Only |q| ≤ 2/λ is reachable (limiting sphere), so dmin ≥ λ/2 [3, 5]. Convention: |k| = 1/λ (physics texts often use 2π/λ).

Crystal versus single molecule

Icrystal(q) = |Fmol(q)|² · |Σn e2πi q·rn|²

The lattice sum is N² at reciprocal-lattice points and near zero elsewhere, so Bragg peaks sample the molecular transform with a gain of order N (number of unit cells). A single molecule gives the same transform without that gain, which is why its continuous pattern needs vastly higher fluence to record [6]. The detector intensities here are a display model: |F|² of the toy molecule (lysozyme preset), F from atom positions with Z·exp(−s²) form factors (NaCl, Si, Fe, simple cubic), or Wilson-distributed random values (other presets), all × exp(−B s²/2).

Smearing and the rotation method

Bandwidth spreads the sphere into a shell bounded by radii 1/λmax and 1/λmin, all passing through O. Divergence rotates the sphere about O; mosaic spread rotates the lattice about O, which smears each point into an arc of length η|d*|. All three give zero width at O, growing with |d*|. The angular acceptance η + δ + (Δλ/λ) tan θB follows from differentiating Bragg's law; it treats divergence as in-plane and ignores the Lorentz factor. Spinning about φ sweeps points through the sphere; points near the spindle axis that never cross it form the blind region [5]. Spot position on a flat detector: r = D tan 2θ. Photon energy E [keV] = 12.398 / λ [Å] [7].
